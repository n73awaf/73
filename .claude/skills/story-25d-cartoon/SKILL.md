# Cozy 2.5D Outlined Cartoon Storytelling Style

## Core Aesthetic & Visual Rules
- **Art Style:** Cozy 2.5D / simplified 3D environment with clean dark outlines (inked/cel-shaded feel) paired with cute, rounded minimalist characters and expressive simple faces.
- **Geometric & Organic Balance:** Keep characters, birds, and props rounded, friendly, and easy to read; use subtle low-poly/faceted shading on trees, hills, rocks, and small objects.
- **Color & Mood Shifts:** Adapt palette and lighting to the emotional beat of each scene:
  - *Daylight / Nostalgia:* Soft sky blues, warm wood/wall tones, gentle greens.
  - *Winter / Melancholy:* Cool desaturated blues, muted brick/snow tones, soft falling particles.
  - *Night / Flashback:* Deep indigo/purple skies, glowing headlights/lamps, moody contrast.
- **Atmosphere & Depth:** Include subtle depth-of-field blur on background/foreground layers and soft floating dust/sparkle motes in indoor scenes when appropriate.

## Motion & Camera Pacing
- Favor smooth, deliberate camera movements (slow push-ins, gentle pans, and tracking shots) over jarring cuts.
- Keep character and animal animations clean and readable: subtle breathing/bobbing, wing flaps, head tilts, and symbolic repeated actions/sounds.
- Maintain clear framing in vertical (9:16) safe zones so bottom-center Arabic captions never collide with key visual subjects or faces.

## Camera Movement & Shot Transitions
- **Match motion to emotional beat:** Use slow push-ins for emotional realizations, clues, or key objects; use smooth tracking or high-angle pans for travel, distance, or setting establishment.
- **Keep camera speeds measured:** Prefer steady, unhurried camera motion that lets viewers read both the visual action and bottom Arabic captions comfortably.
- **Use purposeful cuts:** Rely on clean hard cuts timed to narration beats, reserving bright or flash transitions for memory shifts and emotional turns.
- **Avoid shaky or erratic motion:** Keep framing stable and legible across vertical (9:16) shots.

## Visual Storytelling & Symbolism
- Break scripts into beat-by-beat visual shots tied directly to voiceover lines.
- Plant visual motifs early (objects, colors, drawings, sounds, or companion characters) and pay them off emotionally later in the story.
- Keep every visual prompt clean and focused on composition, lighting, environment, character pose/expression, and camera motion—leaving final Arabic typography/captions to post-production or caption layers.

## Output Format When Generating Scenes
For each scene/beat, provide:
1. **Timestamp / Beat & Narration Line**
2. **Visual & Environment Description** (palette, lighting, outlines, props)
3. **Character / Subject Pose & Action**
4. **Camera & Motion Note** (framing, movement, atmospheric effects)
